# Portfolio Review — Open Doors Preparation

This repository is a record of practical cybersecurity work. The goal of this document is to keep future additions technically useful, reproducible, and honest.

## What makes a strong case study?

Each substantial case study should answer:

1. **Objective** — What security question or problem was being investigated?
2. **Environment** — What systems, services, tools, and versions were used?
3. **Method** — What steps were performed in the authorized lab or platform?
4. **Evidence** — What logs, packets, alerts, screenshots, or artifacts support the result?
5. **Analysis** — What did the evidence show, and why?
6. **Detection/response** — What rule, control, investigation step, or mitigation was used?
7. **Validation** — How was the result tested or reproduced?
8. **Limitations** — What was not tested or what could produce false positives?
9. **Lessons learned** — What technical concept became clearer through the experiment?

### Lower priority

Avoid creating repositories that only contain a certificate, course notes, or generic definitions. Keep certificates as supporting evidence and use repositories to demonstrate what was actually built, tested, analyzed, or learned.

## Evidence standard

Do not claim an experiment, result, score, internship duty, or technical capability unless it was actually completed. Where work is based on TryHackMe, LetsDefend, BTLO, Forage, CodeAlpha, or another provider, identify the provider clearly and distinguish platform work from independent extensions.

## Safe security-testing standard

Offensive tools and attack simulations in this portfolio are for self-owned, intentionally vulnerable, or explicitly authorized environments. No repository content should imply authorization to test third-party systems.
